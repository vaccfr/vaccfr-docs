## C1 Enroute Core Competencies (C1–C7)

The core competencies define what a C1 Enroute controller must master at CPT level. They are derived from the French vACC CBTA framework — the same framework used by the S3 syllabus — and consistent with the VATEUD CPT Log items.

> **Naming note:** C1–C7 below are **competency codes** from the vACC CBTA framework. Everywhere else in this document set, "C1" on its own refers to the **VATSIM Enroute rating**. Where ambiguity is possible, competencies are cited with their name (e.g. "C4 — Workload Management").

| Competency | Description | Observable Behaviours (OBs) |
|---|---|---|
| **C1 — Communication** | Communicates clearly and efficiently in normal and non-normal situations. | Establishes that the frequency is usable and the pilot is ready before issuing complex clearances · Selects what / when / how to communicate based on workload and urgency · Uses concise, standard phraseology; adapts without becoming informal · Confirms understanding (readback/hearback loop) for safety-critical items · Escalates appropriately when deviations persist |
| **C2 — Situational Awareness** | Maintains an accurate, continuously updated mental model of traffic and emerging threats; anticipates future states. | Anticipates the next 10–15 minutes (enroute horizon: conflicts build across the whole sector) · Detects abnormal patterns early (altitude, route, speed, silent aircraft) · Performs regular sector scans (Avoid / Trap / Mitigate) |
| **C3 — Separation & Safety** | Identifies and applies procedures and applicable regulations to ensure safety. | Applies enroute separation minima consistently, laterally and vertically (crossings, level changes, catch-up) · Uses safety buffers proportional to uncertainty (latency, pilot skill, sim limits) · Prioritises safety-critical tasks over service quality when saturated · Demonstrates disciplined adherence to local SOPs and LOAs |
| **C4 — Workload Management** | Organises, prioritises and manages tasks in line with traffic complexity and operational demand. | Prioritises effectively (airborne traffic over top-down) · Employs workload mitigation strategies (stack, speed reduction, level capping, ground stop) · Applies the 5 Ds: *Do early / Drop / Delay / Delegate / Do* |
| **C5 — Problem Solving & Decision Making** | Identifies precursors, mitigates problems, and makes decisions in a timely manner. | Identifies options and constraints, selects timely actions · Revises the plan when new information appears · Balances flow efficiency against safety margin · Demonstrates resilience facing unexpected events |
| **C6 — Coordination & Teamwork** | Collaborates effectively with adjacent sectors, controllers and pilots to ensure continuity and shared awareness. | Coordinates early and clearly with adjacent centres and underlying positions (XFL, COP, direct routings) · Shares intent with colleagues and pilots · Uses handoffs / point-outs / LOAs correctly and predictably |
| **C7 — Professionalism & Mindset** | Demonstrates composure, discipline and adherence to standards in all situations. | Maintains a calm, respectful tone under pressure · Accepts feedback; identifies own areas of improvement · Is honest and accepts accountability for errors |

> **Note on "tools" and "knowledge":** these are not standalone CBTA competencies. Knowledge is a prerequisite (Block 1 self-study), and radar tool mastery is treated as a **session-exit standard in 2.1** (not CBTA-graded, no intermediate gate inside the session). Session 2.1 is a merged session: its first part refreshes the toolset and the fundamentals (separation minima, conflict groups, route extension) before the flat-separation exercises — a trainee still fighting the interface at the end of 2.1 cannot enter the subsequent competency-focused sessions.

## Objective Descriptors — reference matrix

Each competency targeted by a session receives a **descriptor** defining the expected trainer input and exit standard. Taken from the IATA CBTA model (ch. 3.1.1.6.3.2) adapted for ATC training.

| Descriptor | Definition | Standard required | Trainer input |
|---|---|---|---|
| Establish | Set base for understanding or performance | N/A | Considerable |
| Observe | Trainer demonstrates performance | N/A | Considerable |
| Familiarise | Trainer guides initial development | N/A | Considerable |
| Develop | Improve understanding or performance | Competent | Considerable |
| Practice | Trainee action to improve performance | Competent | Moderate |
| Consolidate | Reinforce understanding or performance | Competent | Moderate |
| Complete | Meet required performance standard | Competent | Moderate |
| Demonstrate | Show competent understanding or performance | Competent | Minimal |
| Apply | Use competencies to manage | Competent | Minimal |

> **Reading note:** "Competent" in the Standard column refers to level 3 of the grading scale. It is the **minimum standard required** at session exit for that descriptor.

**Learning Objectives format per session** (IATA): **Task** (the competency-based task to perform) · **Conditions** (the conditions under which the task is performed, per session guide) · **Standard** (defined by the objective descriptor and the OBs of the relevant competency).

## Competency Grading Scale (1 to 5)

The 1–5 grading scale defines what each competency level **means**. It is used for **progression gates** and **structured assessments** where a level needs to be named (e.g. "Competent on C3").

**Training sessions are not graded session-by-session on this scale.** Day-to-day, mentors record a session-level outcome instead (OA / OWAP / ONA). The 1–5 scale remains the underlying language: when a descriptor requires the "Competent" standard, that means level 3 as defined here.

The scale evaluates competency on four combined dimensions — the final level equals the **lowest of the four**.

| Grade | Level | How well | How many OBs | How often | Outcome (TEM) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Expert | Exemplary | All, almost all | Always, almost always | Highly effective — enhances safety |
| 4 | Advanced | Effectively | Most | Regularly* | Promotes and maintains effective ops |
| 3 | Competent | Adequately | Many | Regularly | Minimum standard — effective ops |
| 2 | Not yet competent | Minimum acceptable | Some | Occasionally | Below standard — intervention needed |
| 1 | Not competent | Ineffectively | Few, hardly any | Rarely | Detrimental — repeat needed |

> **Aggregation rule:** the final competency grade equals the **lowest level among the four dimensions**. A trainee who demonstrated most OBs but had one situation reducing safety margins will be graded at the level matching that outcome.

**Minimum standard for descriptor validation.** At the end of a session whose descriptor requires the "Competent" standard, the trainee must reach a **minimum level 3** on the targeted competencies. Descriptors without a standard (Establish, Observe, Familiarise) require no formal grading.

**VATEUD CPT Log grid (examination only)** — used for the mock CPT (6.1) and the formal VATEUD CPT:

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| N/A | Not applicable or not observed during the session |
| I | Insufficient — comment mandatory |
| S | Sufficient — expected level |
| G | Good — above expected level |

## Training session outcomes (OA / OWAP / ONA)

For all training sessions (Blocks 2 through 5, and session 6.3), mentors record a **session-level outcome**, not a 1–5 grade per competency.

| Outcome | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **OA** | Objective Achieved | The trainee reached the standard required by the session's descriptor, with the level of mentor input the descriptor planned for. Expected outcome of a well-paced session. |
| **OWAP** | Objective Achieved With additional Practice | The trainee reached the standard, but required **more mentor input than the descriptor planned for**. **OWAP is a pass** — a tracking signal, not a flag against the trainee. |
| **ONA** | Objective Not Achieved | The standard was not reached by session end. Triggers a session repeat or targeted catch-up before progression. |

**Why OWAP exists — input vs. standard.** Each session's descriptor specifies two things at once: the expected exit standard (e.g. Competent), *and* the expected level of mentor input to reach it (Considerable / Moderate / Minimal). OA and OWAP both represent passing the standard — they differ only in whether the input matched the plan.

> **Mentors give as much input as needed to achieve the objective.** Mentor input is not rationed — under-coaching to "protect the OA" is the wrong reflex. If the trainee needs more help, give it. The session is then graded OWAP, the trainee passes, and the system has a useful data point.

**What OWAP signals.** A single OWAP is normal. It only matters in aggregate: the same competency OWAP'd across multiple sessions suggests a targeted catch-up for that trainee; the same competency OWAP'd across multiple trainees suggests a curriculum-level adjustment (Training Department signal). Under-reporting OWAP blinds the system.

**Mapping outcomes to descriptors:**

| Descriptor type | What OA means | What OWAP means |
|---|---|---|
| Establish / Observe / Familiarise (no standard) | Trainee exposed to the competency, planned considerable input delivered | *Not applicable* — OA or ONA only |
| Develop (Competent · considerable input) | Competent reached with planned considerable input | Competent reached but input went further beyond "considerable" than expected |
| Practice / Consolidate / Complete (Competent · moderate input) | Competent reached with moderate input | Competent reached but input drifted toward "considerable" |
| Demonstrate / Apply (Competent · minimal input) | Competent reached with minimal input | Competent reached but input drifted toward "moderate" |

> **Block 6 specifics:** 6.1 and 6.2 are graded with the VATEUD CPT Log grid (N/A / I / S / G). The VATEUD theory exam is pass / fail per VATEUD rules. Session 6.3 reverts to OA / OWAP / ONA under the Apply descriptor.

> **C1-specific readings (per CPT Log Guidelines v1.1):** item 8 → sequence built with speed control and/or vectoring, ideally within +2 NM of local procedure separation or as coordinated with Approach/Tower; spacing may be increased when workload justifies (point merge feed per LOA). Item 10 → services matched to the airspace class the traffic is in. Item 11 → conflicts spotted in due time and solved with intermediate levels, vectors or speed techniques — this is the core skill trained in Blocks 2–3.

> **C1 CPT conditions (per VATEUD CPT policy):** two examiners are required for a C1 CPT. TWR may be online; APP must be standby and ready to log on as instructed by the examiner. Session 6.1 (mock CPT) replicates these conditions. Per the Log Guidelines: a CPT cannot be failed if no competency is insufficient; any Insufficient requires a written comment; insufficient *professional behaviour* (item 3) alone means the CPT cannot be passed.

## C1 Pathway (flowchart summary)

**Entry:** Qualified S3 candidate — 50h APP + 5h/6 rolling months.

**Block 1 — Theory preparation (self-study).** Airspace & sectorisation · SOPs · LOAs · Tools · Phraseology.

**Block 2 — Enroute Radar Basics (Sweatbox, agnostic sectors) — 3 sessions.**

- **2.1 Tools + Flat separation** *(merged session — agnostic sector BB / RR / EE)* — tool refreshers (SEP, conflict groups, route extension) then flat separation across geometries (90°, ± parallel, converging), equal then varied speeds. **Tool competence at exit.** Practice C1/C3 · Develop C2.
- **2.2 Level crossings** *(agnostic sector BB / RR / EE)* — climb / descent / parallel FL change. Practice C1 · Develop C2/C3.
- **2.3 Catch-up + Sequencing** *(PAR_CTR — BIBAX/LUKIP point merge)* — overtakes, speed control, merge feed. Practice C1/C2/C3 · Familiarise C4/C5.

**GATE Block 2** — Enroute basics competent (simple scenarios).

**Block 3 — Building to C1 Standard (Sweatbox, training sector) — 2 sessions.** *Coordination is integrated across both sessions (crossings, XFL/COP changes, stack) — no dedicated session.*

- **3.1 Sector flows + crossing hotspot** — general flow with the sector's crossing hotspot; STCA last-minute avoidance. Practice C2/C3/C5 · Develop C6.
- **3.2 Evolving load + stack** *(2nd trainer / pseudo-pilot required)* — frequency/load management as traffic builds, stack, secondary airfield. Doubles as pre-live consolidation. Consolidate C1–C3 · Practice C4/C6.

**GATE Block 3** — Competent on all C (mentor consensus).

**Block 4 — Live sessions with mentor — 2 to 3 sessions (adaptive).**

- **4.1 Coached live** — no top-down, active mentor. Complete all C.
- **4.1b Optional 2nd live** — no top-down, evening/weekend for traffic density; scheduled only on mentor decision after 4.1. Complete all C.
- **4.2 Live with top-down** — silent observer. Complete C3/C4 · Demonstrate others.

**GATE Block 4** — Solo release authorised. → **SOLO RELEASE**

**Block 5 — Solo (single phase).**

- **Solo phase** — single phase, no event/no-event distinction. Minimum 30 days of solo (spreadable over several weeks). Trainee alone on the network, self-reports to reference mentor. Apply all C.

**GATE Block 5** — Ready for pre-CPT preparation.

**Block 6 — CPT Preparation (load specified per session, up to MAX-load sim).**

- **6.1 Mock CPT** — VATEUD conditions, graded N/A / I / S / G.
- **6.2 Remediation or confirmation** — adaptive based on 6.1; load specified per session, up to a MAX-load sim (sweatbox).
- **VATEUD theory exam** — pass/fail on the VATEUD platform.
- **6.3 Final pre-CPT solo currency** — 30 effective solo days (not calendar), evolving load building toward sector maximum, stack opening expected when load requires. Apply all C.
- **CPT recommendation (mentor + DFA)** — unlocks formal CPT scheduling.

**VATEUD C1 CPT** — formal examination, two examiners (TWR may be online, APP standby). → **C1 rating obtained.**

## Sectors used per session

Sector choice follows the same principle as the S3 syllabus: **we train competency, not sector**. Block 2 uses agnostic sectors to focus on the fundamentals; Block 3 applies them to the candidate's **training sector**.

| Session | Sector | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 Tools + Flat separation | Agnostic — LFBB, LFRR or LFEE (TBD) | Merged session: tool competence at session exit (SEP refreshers, conflict groups, route extension) + flat separation across geometries (90°, ± parallel, converging), equal then varied speeds |
| 2.2 Level crossings | Agnostic — same platform as 2.1 | Vertical management on known geometry: climbs and descents through occupied levels, parallel traffic requiring FL change |
| 2.3 Catch-up + Sequencing | PAR_CTR | Overtake management and sequencing on the BIBAX/LUKIP point merge: speed control, delay absorption, merge feeding |
| 3.1 Sector flows + hotspot + STCA | Training sector | Local flows and the sector's crossing hotspot are the subject; scripted STCA scenario for last-minute avoidance. Coordination integrated (XFL/COP) |
| 3.2 Evolving load + stack + secondary airfield | Training sector | Frequency and load management as traffic builds; local stack; secondary airfield. Hard requirement: 2nd trainer or pseudo-pilot. Doubles as pre-live consolidation |
| Block 4 (live) | Training sector | Real traffic on the qualification sector; 4.1/4.1b without top-down (underlying positions staffed where possible), 4.2 with full top-down |
| Block 5 (solo) | Training sector | Single solo phase on the qualification sector — no event/no-event distinction |
| Block 6 — 6.1 / 6.2 | Training sector | 6.1 identical to formal CPT conditions (two examiners at the real CPT; TWR may be online, APP standby); 6.2 load specified per session, up to a MAX-load sim |
| Block 6 — 6.3 Pre-CPT solo currency | Training sector | 30 effective solo days · Final currency before recommendation · Load building toward sector maximum |

*The Block 2 agnostic platform will be confirmed later — **LFBB, LFRR or LFEE** — except session 2.3, fixed on **PAR_CTR** for the BIBAX/LUKIP point merge. "Training sector" means the candidate's qualification sector; the mentor adapts those sessions accordingly.*
