S3 Approach Core Competencies (C1–C7)

The core competencies define what an S3 Approach controller must master at CPT level. They are derived from the French vACC CBTA framework and consistent with the VATEUD CPT Log items.

CompetencyDescriptionObservable Behaviours (OBs)
C1 — Communication Communicates clearly and efficiently in normal and non-normal situations.
  • Establishes that the frequency is usable and the pilot is ready before issuing complex clearances
  • Selects what / when / how to communicate based on workload and urgency
  • Uses concise, standard phraseology; adapts without becoming informal
  • Confirms understanding (readback/hearback loop) for safety-critical items
  • Escalates appropriately when deviations persist
C2 — Situational Awareness Maintains an accurate, continuously updated mental model of traffic and emerging threats; anticipates future states.
  • Anticipates the next 5–10 minutes
  • Detects abnormal patterns early (altitude, route, speed, silent aircraft)
  • Performs regular sector scans (Avoid / Trap / Mitigate)
C3 — Separation & Safety Identifies and applies procedures and applicable regulations to ensure safety.
  • Applies separation rules consistently
  • Uses safety buffers proportional to uncertainty (latency, pilot skill, sim limits)
  • Prioritises safety-critical tasks over service quality when saturated
  • Demonstrates disciplined adherence to local SOPs
C4 — Workload Management Organises, prioritises and manages tasks in line with traffic complexity and operational demand.
  • Prioritises effectively (ATC equivalent of aviate–navigate–communicate: airborne traffic over top-down)
  • Employs workload mitigation strategies (holding, speed reduction, ground stop)
  • Applies the 5 Ds: Do early / Drop / Delay / Delegate / Do
C5 — Problem Solving & Decision Making Identifies precursors, mitigates problems, and makes decisions in a timely manner.
  • Identifies options and constraints, selects timely actions
  • Revises the plan when new information appears
  • Balances flow efficiency against safety margin
  • Demonstrates resilience facing unexpected events
C6 — Coordination & Teamwork Collaborates effectively with adjacent sectors, controllers and pilots to ensure continuity and shared awareness.
  • Coordinates early and clearly with adjacent sectors and positions
  • Shares intent with colleagues and pilots
  • Uses handoffs / point-outs / LOAs correctly and predictably
C7 — Professionalism & Mindset Demonstrates composure, discipline and adherence to standards in all situations.
  • Maintains a calm, respectful tone under pressure
  • Accepts feedback; identifies own areas of improvement
  • Is honest and accepts accountability for errors
Note on "tools" and "knowledge": these are not standalone CBTA competencies. Knowledge is a prerequisite (Block 1 self-study), and radar tool mastery is treated as a session-exit standard in 2.1 (not CBTA-graded, no intermediate gate inside the session) — a trainee still fighting the interface at the end of 2.1 cannot enter the subsequent competency-focused sessions.

Learning Objectives format per session

Following the IATA format, each session formulates its objectives along three elements:

ElementDescription
TaskThe competency-based task to perform
ConditionsThe conditions under which the task is performed (per session guide)
StandardThe standard to achieve, defined by the objective descriptor and the OBs of the relevant competency

Competency Grading Scale (1 to 5)

The 1–5 grading scale defines what each competency level means. It is used for progression gates and structured assessments where a level needs to be named (e.g. "Competent on C3").

Training sessions are not graded session-by-session on this scale. Day-to-day, mentors record a session-level outcome instead — see the next page (Training session outcomes: OA / OWAP / ONA). The 1–5 scale remains the underlying language: when a descriptor requires the "Competent" standard, that means level 3 as defined here.

The scale evaluates competency on four combined dimensions — the final level equals the lowest of the four.

Grade Level How well How many OBs How often Outcome (TEM)
5ExpertExemplaryAll, almost allAlways, almost alwaysHighly effective — enhances safety
4AdvancedEffectivelyMostRegularly*Promotes and maintains effective ops
3CompetentAdequatelyManyRegularlyMinimum standard — effective ops
2Not yet competentMinimum acceptableSomeOccasionallyBelow standard — intervention needed
1Not competentIneffectivelyFew, hardly anyRarelyDetrimental — repeat needed
Aggregation rule: the final competency grade equals the lowest level among the four dimensions (How well, How many, How often, Outcome). A trainee who demonstrated most OBs but had one situation reducing safety margins will be graded at the level matching that outcome.

Minimum standard for descriptor validation

At the end of a session whose descriptor requires the "Competent" standard, the trainee must reach a minimum level 3 on the targeted competencies. Descriptors without a standard (Establish, Observe, Familiarise) require no formal grading.

Training session outcomes (OA / OWAP / ONA)

For all training sessions (Blocks 2 through 5, and session 6.3), mentors record a session-level outcome, not a 1–5 grade per competency. Three outcomes are possible:

Outcome Name Meaning
OA Objective Achieved The trainee reached the standard required by the session's descriptor, with the level of mentor input the descriptor planned for. This is the expected outcome of a well-paced session.
OWAP Objective Achieved With additional Practice The trainee reached the standard, but required more mentor input than the descriptor planned for. OWAP is a pass — the objective was met. It is a tracking signal, not a flag against the trainee.
ONA Objective Not Achieved The standard was not reached by session end. Triggers a session repeat or targeted catch-up before progression.

Why OWAP exists — input vs. standard

Each session's descriptor specifies two things at once: the expected exit standard (e.g. Competent), and the expected level of mentor input to reach it (Considerable / Moderate / Minimal). OA and OWAP both represent passing the standard — they differ only in whether the input matched the plan.

Mentors give as much input as needed to achieve the objective. Mentor input is not rationed — under-coaching to "protect the OA" is the wrong reflex. If the trainee needs more help on a given session, give it. The session is then graded OWAP, the trainee passes, and the system has a useful data point.

What OWAP signals

A single OWAP is normal — every trainee has one or two over a pathway. It only matters in aggregate:

  • Same competency OWAP'd across multiple sessions → suggests the trainee may benefit from a targeted catch-up session on that competency before continuing.
  • Same competency OWAP'd across multiple trainees → suggests a curriculum-level adjustment may be needed (descriptor too ambitious for that session, briefing material insufficient, scenario design issue). This is a Training Department signal, not a mentor signal.

The OWAP scheme is what makes that monitoring possible. Do not avoid it out of mis-placed kindness to the trainee — under-reporting OWAP blinds the system.

Airfields used per session

Airfield choice follows a simple principle: we train competency, not airfield. Block 2 (Radar Basics) uses non-specific airfields (LFPO, LFMN) to focus on the fundamentals; Block 3 (Building to S3 Standard) applies those fundamentals to the candidate's training airfield.

SessionAirfieldRationale
2.1 Radar tools + SequenceLFPO (non-specific)Combined session: tool competence at session exit + sequence-building. LFPO offers flat, readable terrain to focus on the toolkit, not the airfield
2.2 Vectoring + SeparationLFPO (non-specific)Vectoring technique is airfield-agnostic. Separation skills built on the same non-specific platform
2.3 Point Merge familiarisationLFMN (non-specific)Point Merge is the reference platform for the technique in France
3.1 Flows and proceduresTraining airfieldLocal SIDs/STARs and flows are the subject
3.2 Holding mechanicsTraining airfieldLocal holding patterns and capacity
3.3 Holding decision + loadTraining airfieldDecision depends on local capacity. Hard requirement: 2nd trainer or pseudo-pilot

3.5 Mixed trafficTraining airfieldFinal integration close to live
