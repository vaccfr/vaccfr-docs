# ATC Training Manual

## WORKING DOCUMENT

New Edition

Introducing Competency-Based Training

Based on the latest IATA recommendation for pilot training. Adapted to ATC Training on VATSIM

The fundamental principle of Competency-Based Training and Assessment (CBTA) is the recognition that it is neither practical nor effective to train for every possible operational scenario. 
Rather than focusing on specific events, training is designed to develop the controller’s core competencies, ensuring they can manage both expected and unforeseen situations safely and effectively.

To implement Competency-Based Training we need to define the following elements.

A) The core competency that we need ATC students to master by the end of their training

B) Within these competencies, define what are the Observable Behaviors (OBs) that define the said competency.
OBs are always positive (eg. Remains calm in all circumstances NOT Loses his calm under pressure)

This will take some time to be refined and might be delayed for later implementation.

C) Define objectives to be achieved for each session leading to the CPT. Build a course footprint and lesson plans for each session.

D) Use objective descriptors such as “Apply / Practice / Demonstrate” and refer to a matrix for the amount of trainer input needed and the expected outcome (needs to be competent or not)








## A, Core Competencies S3 Approach Controller (proposal)

### C1 — Communication 

**Description:** Communicates clearly and efficiently in normal and non-normal situations.

- Establishes that the frequency is usable and the pilot is ready before issuing complex clearances
- Selects what/when/how to communicate based on workload and urgency
- Uses concise, standard phraseology; adapts without becoming informal when needed
- Confirms understanding (readback/hearback loop) for safety-critical elements
- Uses appropriate escalation when deviations persist <br>

### C2 — Situational Awareness 

- Maintains an accurate and continuously updated mental model of the traffic situation, operational environment, constraints, and developing threats. Anticipates future states and detects deviations early to prevent undesired situations.
- Anticipates next 5–10 minutes 
- Detects abnormal patterns early (wrong altitude, wrong route, unexpected speed, silent aircraft)
- Performs regular sector scans (Avoid / Trap / Mitigate)


### C3 — Separation & Safety Assurance (ATC Procedures)

- Identifies and applies appropriate procedures in accordance with published operating instructions and applicable regulations.
- Applies separation rules consistently
- Uses safety buffers proportional to uncertainty (latency, pilot skill, sim limitations)
- Prioritizes safety-critical tasks over service quality when saturated
- Demonstrates disciplined adherence to local SOPs


### C4 — Workload Management

- Organizes, prioritizes, and manages tasks effectively in accordance with traffic complexity and operational demands. Allocates attention appropriately and uses strategies to maintain control during high workload situations.
- Prioritizes tasks effectively (aviate–navigate–communicate equivalent for ATC) >> Prioritizes flying traffic over top-down
- Employs workload mitigation strategies effectively (implements holdings, reduces speed, forces a ground stop on departure) >> 5D’s: Do early / Drop / Delay / Delegate / Do

### C5 — Problem Solving and Decision Making 

- Identifies precursors, mitigates problems; and makes decisions
- Identifies options, constraints, and selects timely actions
- Revises the plan when new information appears
- Balances flow efficiency with safety margin
- Demonstrates resilience when encountering an unexpected event

### C6 — Coordination & Teamwork 

- Collaborates effectively with adjacent sectors, controllers, and pilots to ensure continuity and shared situational awareness. 
- Coordinates early and clearly with adjacent sectors/positions
- Shares intent to colleagues and pilots alike (what is the plan)
- Uses handoffs/point-outs/LOAs correctly and predictably

### C7 — Professionalism & Learning Mindset

- Demonstrates composure, discipline, and adherence to standards in all situations
- Maintains calm, respectful tone under pressure
- Accepts feedback; self-identifies improvement areas
- Is honest and accept accountability when an error is made.
