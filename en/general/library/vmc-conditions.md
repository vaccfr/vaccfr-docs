# VMC Conditions

In order to fly under Visual Flight Rules (VFR), certain weather minimas apply, these are called : **V**isual **M**eteorological **C**onditions.
They are defined by both visibility and distance relative to clouds (lateraly and verticaly).

## VMC in F and G airspaces

**When at and below the highest of the 2 levels, 3000 ft AMSL or 1000 ft ASFC**: the minimum flight visibility is 1500m or 5000m if Indicated Air Speed is greater than 140kts.

It is the responsibilty of the pilot in command to remain out of clouds and keep the surface in sight.

**When at and above the highest of the 2 levels, 3000 ft AMSL or 1000 ft ASFC**: the minimum flight visibility is 5000m up to FL100, above FL100 it is 8000m.

The pilot in command is responsible of mainting 1500 m horizontally and 300 m (1000 ft) vertically from clouds.

## VMC in A, B, C, D and E airspaces

Within all controlled airspaces and when above FL100: the minimum flight visibility is 8000m.

The pilot in command is responsible for maintaining 1500 m horizontally and 300 m (1000 ft) vertically from clouds.

## Exception : Special VFR

In some cases, the pilot mais be required to depart without all VMC minimas present. For example : a medivac rotorcraft can not wait for the weather to clear up before saving someone.

In this case, if horizontal visibility is still greater than 1500m, the pilot may obtain a special VFR clearance, clearing him to depart.
