# MILITARY ATC PROCEDURES

## APPLICABILITY
This qualification session aims to give VATSIM ATC controllers the ability to control airfields part of the TIER-2 Designation set by French vACC.
Only controllers having a VATSIM rating as controller shall be allowed to control a military airfield and according to their actual rating:
- S2 controllers for TWR position
- S3 controller for APP position.

## REFERENCE DOCUMENTATION
The following documents must be known, understood and applied by controllers holding a position in France to ensure that they respect all military procedures and associated reference points.

### MIAM (Military Aeronautical Information Manual)
The manual MIAM groups the general information of military regulations. This information is broken down in 3 sections:
- MIAM GEN (Generalities)
- MIAM ENRT (Enroute)
- MIAM AERODROMES

### MIAC 1 (Military Instrument Approach Chart)
The manual MIAC 1 is a compilation of instrument procedure charts for state aircraft on civil aviation aerodromes.
This document relates to instrument approach procedures for airplanes and helicopters.

### MIAC 2 (Military Instrument Approach Chart) 
The manual MIAC 2 is a compilation of instrument procedure maps for state aircraft on defense aerodromes - <u>Conventional Procedures: Airplanes and Helicopters and Non-Conventional Procedures: Helicopters.</u>

 ### MIAC 4 (Military Instrument Approach Chart)
The manual MIAC 4  is a compilation of instrument procedures and approaches for military aircraft and combat and training aircraft.

### AVUE FRANCE
The AVUE FRANCE manual is a compilation of visual procedure maps for aircraft and helicopters.


## CONTROL TOWER (S2 Rating)
Control Tower shall exercise control of all aircraft operating to, from, or on the airfield and within the CTR between the surface and the ceiling specified in the chart.
Pilots in VFR shall contact the TWR controller at the entry/exit point at the latest.

Tower controlling high performance jets have to remember that Jet Circuit is slightly different from Conventional pattern, since it starts from INITIAL POINT and not from DOWNWIND. For that reason, the Controller should ensure that he is aware of the 2 different reference documents available in the DIRCAM MIAC4 for Fighter jets and AVUE FRANCE (military airfields) for Aircraft and Helicopters.

It is also worth noting that while fighter jets under OAT flight plans will use dedicated procedures, some activities can also be conducted under GAT. Controllers will therefore be expected to know their way around the civilian French AIP to look for the appropriate
charts, procedures, and reporting points.

Prior approval from the tower shall be obtained for all taxi, takeoff, landing, towing, and related operations.

## WEATHER INFORMATION
### Airfield color states
Terrain color codes are based solely on meteorological parameters. The influence of these minimums is now expressed via the HOTEL suffix.
It is up to each employment authority to define the rules applicable to its aircraft. The color of the field is defined by the most penalizing of the following two values:
- cloud base
- visibility

(tableau des couleurs)

### Suffixes
Four color suffixes are used to specify the conditions of arrival in the field:
- BLACK: runway unusable for technical or safety reasons.
- X.RAY: a radar guided approach is not feasible.
- SPECIAL: one or more special procedures are in effect. (Barrier inoperative, no refueling available etc...)
- HOTEL: the weather minima are lower than the minima of the best final ground average available. This suffix is applicable for colors other than RED.

## FORMATION FLIGHTS
Formation flights shall be controlled/cleared as a single aircraft unless the formation leader requests otherwise.
Radio communication flow is strictly between the formation leader and the controller.
The controller should expect a radio roll call from all wingmen before being contacted by the leader.
<p align="center"> i.e: NATO11 check... 12... 13... 14. Avord Tower NATO11</p> 

Should a call be addressed to a specific wingman, the controller must use the aircraft callsign including the sequence number. (i.e: NATO13 Avord Tower squawk stand-by).
The responsibility for landing interval between elements of a formation flight rests with the pilots in the formation.
