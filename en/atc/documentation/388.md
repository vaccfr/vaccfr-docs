# Introduction
Although the A380 is an aircraft like any other, its wingspan and weight require slightly specific handling both in the tower and on radar. This is a summary sheet. For further details, refer to the AIP and the relevant documentation.

# General information

The **L’A380-800** (ultimately the only version produced) is a four‑engine aircraft available in two variants:
- the A380-841, equipped with four Rolls‑Royce Trent 900 engines
- the A380‑861, equipped with four Engine Alliance GP7200 engines (a joint venture between PW and GE)

## Dimensions

Its wings measure $79.676$m, with a total wing area of $848.5 m^2$

![dimension_388.png](/dimension_388.png)

## Mass

Here are the aircraft’s characteristic weights that are useful to know:

- MTOW (Maximum Takeoff Weight): $575 000$ kg (versions with an enhanced thrust package)
- MLW (Maximum Landing Weight) : $395 000$ kg

Maximum usable fuel: $323 546$L i.e. $253 983$ kg with a SG (Specific Gravity) of $0.785$. 


# Aerodrome management

## Taxi and parking

**Due to its wingspan**, numerous taxiing limitations apply to the A380.
It belongs to aerodrome **reference Code F**, just like the B748 (wingspan between 65 m and 80 m).

These limitations may range from restrictions on the use of parallel taxiways (for example, limiting a taxiway to Code C aircraft while the A380 is passing) to a complete prohibition of using a given taxiway (for instance, at CDG, taxiway E between N and B is prohibited to the A380).

These restrictions are published in the AIP, and dedicated charts are available.

In France, three airports are capable of accommodating the A380:

- LFPG (Emirates, Etihad, Qatar, Air France, Korean Air, Singapore)
- LFMN (Emirates) 
- LFBO (Toutes les compagnies exploitantes)

Several other airfields serve as diversion airports, sometimes with additional specific restrictions.
- LFPO (MTOW = MLW = 398t)
- LFBD
- LFSB

In most cases, using a parking stand for the A380 results in restrictions on the adjacent stands (e.g., stands CFW and CFE at Terminal 2C in CDG).

## Wake turbulence and takeoff separation

**The A380’s wake turbulence category** is **J** for Super (ICAO) or A under RECAT‑EU.

Departure time‑based separation:

# Radar management

On approach, **the A380 is an Approach Category C aircraft**, just like the A320. It uses the same minima and flies at very similar speeds.

In terms of radar separation, however, note that RECAT‑EU increases the required spacing compared with the ICAO standard.
