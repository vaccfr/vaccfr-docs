# Air Traffic Services

There are 3 types of services provided: **Air Traffic Control Service**, **Flight Information Service**, and **Alerting Service**. Each of these services has its own specific missions.

## Air Traffic Control Service

- Prevent collisions between aircraft.
- Organize and optimize air traffic flow.

## Flight Information Service

This service provides aircraft with all advice and information useful for the safe and efficient conduct of flights:
- Weather information.
- Presence of restricted / dangerous areas.
- Information on the aircraft’s position and route followed.
- Information about surrounding traffic.
- Suggested maneuvers to reach a point or trajectory, or to help prevent a collision.
- Information on the runway in use, winds, etc.

## Alerting Service

This involves alerting rescue services when an aircraft requires assistance. 

# Airspaces

Airspaces are three-dimensional volumes (horizontal and vertical, see images below). They are used to determine the level of air traffic services in a given area. There are two main categories of airspace: **controlled airspace** and **uncontrolled airspace**. As the name suggests, controlled airspace is an area where air traffic control service (separation between aircraft) is provided, as opposed to uncontrolled airspace where separation between aircraft is not provided.

![vfr_and_airspaces.png](/vfr_and_airspaces.png)
![decoupe_verticale_mus.png](/decoupe_verticale_mus.png)

There are several types of controlled and uncontrolled airspace. These are called airspace “classes.” Each class defines the level of service provided and the pilot’s obligations. For example, to enter certain areas, prior authorization may be required.

In the first chapter, we saw the difference between separation (air traffic control service) and traffic information (flight information service). The table below summarizes this information for the different airspace classes.

|  Class    | Traffic type | Service provided and traffic information                           | Radio / subject to clearance
|:----------:|:---------------:|:-------------------------------------------------------------:|:---------------:|
|   **A**    |      IFR        | __Separation__ : IFR vs IFR                                   | Yes |   
|   **C**    |   IFR /VFR      | __Separation__ : IFR vs IFR __Info__: VFR vs VFR              | Yes |
|   **D**    |   IFR /VFR      | __Separation__ : IFR vs IFR __Info__: IFR vs VFR / VFR vs VFR | Yes |
|   **E**    |   IFR /VFR      | __Separation__ : IFR vs IFR __Info__: IFR vs VFR / VFR vs VFR | No |
|   **G**    |   IFR /VFR      | __Separation__ : Aucune __Info__: On request                | No |

**Note:** We intentionally do not mention Class B and F airspace here, as they do not exist in France. In Class C, separation between IFR and special VFR is also provided.

For certain classes of airspace, it is necessary to obtain authorization (clearance) before entering the area. In the case of an IFR flight, this authorization is “implicitly” granted as part of the IFR clearance. For VFR flights, the pilot must, when required, explicitly request authorization beforehand.

It can also be observed that in Class A, VFR traffic is prohibited. This means that, except for special cases (police services, emergency operations, etc.), no VFR traffic is allowed in that airspace.

As a general rule, Class A airspace is found around very large airports, such as Paris Charles de Gaulle or London Heathrow. Class C is typically found around major airports such as Nice, Geneva, Zurich, etc. Class D is commonly found around smaller regional airports such as Lille, Nantes, Marseille, etc.
